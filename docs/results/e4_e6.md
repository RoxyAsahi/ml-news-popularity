# E4–E6 — Threshold, training-set size, regularisation (RQ3)

Scripts: `scripts/e4_threshold.py` (~20 s), `scripts/e5_learning_curve.py` (~2 min),
`scripts/e6_regularisation.py` (~1.5 min). Chronological split; E4/E5 test on the newest 20%,
E6 uses the 10% validation block only. Tables `results/tables/e4_*`, `e5_*`, `e6_*`.

## E4 — Popularity threshold

Thresholds = 50/75/90/95th percentile of training shares (1,500 / 2,900 / 6,400 / 11,200).
HistGB, all features:

| Percentile | Positives in test | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | PR-AUC ÷ positive rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | 43.5% | 0.733 | 0.680 | 1.56 |
| 75 | 20.6% | 0.729 | 0.399 | 1.94 |
| 90 | 8.3% | 0.738 | 0.188 | 2.27 |
| 95 | 4.0% | 0.740 | 0.098 | 2.45 |

- ROC-AUC barely moves (0.73–0.74), while PR-AUC falls from 0.68 to 0.10. Reporting accuracy or
  ROC-AUC alone hides how hard "will this go viral?" really is.
- Relative to chance, the model is *better* at spotting the top 5% (2.45× lift) than the top 50% (1.56×).
- One regressor on log(shares) used as a score matches the classifiers at the median (ROC-AUC 0.729)
  but falls behind at the 95th percentile (0.703 vs 0.740): a dedicated classifier pays off for the
  extreme tail.
- The test set has only 43.5% positives at the training median — the drift from E1 again.
- Without `kw_*`/`self_reference_*` every number drops by 0.02–0.04 but the pattern is the same.

## E5 — Learning curves

Test ROC-AUC, mean of 5 random subsamples of the training block (all features):

| Training articles | LogReg | kNN | Random forest | HistGB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 158 | 0.571 | 0.599 | 0.644 | 0.599 |
| 951 | 0.677 | 0.678 | 0.704 | 0.680 |
| 3,171 | 0.705 | 0.686 | 0.718 | 0.703 |
| 9,514 | 0.714 | 0.690 | 0.729 | 0.723 |
| 31,714 | 0.717 | 0.693 | 0.735 | 0.737 |

- Random forest is best below ~10k articles; HistGB overtakes only with the full data.
- Logistic regression and kNN plateau after ~3k articles (+0.01 from 3k to 32k); the tree
  ensembles are still improving at 32k.
- HistGB fits its training data almost perfectly up to 3k articles (train AUC ≈ 1.0), a textbook
  high-variance regime; logistic regression's train and test AUC converge by ~3k.
- With full data, logistic regression's test AUC (0.717) is *higher* than its training AUC (0.703):
  newer articles are slightly easier to rank, consistent with E1's monthly curve.

## E6 — Regularisation

- With 28k training articles the penalty hardly matters: validation AUC 0.725–0.736 for any
  L2 C ≥ 1e-4 and any L1 C ≥ 0.01.
- With 1,000 training articles it does: L2 validation AUC rises from 0.679 (C = 100) to 0.709
  (C = 0.003); L1 peaks at 0.714 (C = 0.03, 12 non-zero features). Regularisation matters exactly
  where E5 shows the variance problem.
- The sparsest useful L1 model (C = 0.001, 7 features, validation AUC 0.697) keeps: four channel
  dummies (entertainment, world, tech, social media), `kw_avg_avg`, `is_weekend`, `LDA_02`.
- kNN: validation AUC rises from 0.58 (k = 1) to 0.714 (k = 150–500) and then flattens; the
  default k = 50 is slightly under-smoothed.
