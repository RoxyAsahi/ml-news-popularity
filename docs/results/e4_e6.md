## E4 — Popularity threshold

> Revised for review item R5: models are fit on the training block only, the F1 decision cutoff
> is chosen on the validation block (the first version set it from test-score quantiles), and the
> test block is scored once. 95% bootstrap CIs; classifier vs regressor by paired bootstrap.

Thresholds = 50/75/90/95th percentile of training-block shares (1,500 / 2,900 / 6,500 / 11,300).
HistGB, all features:

| Percentile | Positives in test | ROC-AUC [95% CI] | PR-AUC | PR-AUC ÷ positive rate | F1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | 3,447 (43.5%) | 0.731 [0.719, 0.743] | 0.673 | 1.55 | 0.658 |
| 75 | 1,637 (20.6%) | 0.726 [0.712, 0.739] | 0.392 | 1.90 | 0.460 |
| 90 | 638 (8.0%) | 0.729 [0.710, 0.747] | 0.179 | 2.23 | 0.240 |
| 95 | 316 (4.0%) | 0.739 [0.712, 0.764] | 0.097 | 2.43 | 0.171 |

- ROC-AUC stays within 0.726–0.739 (overlapping intervals) while PR-AUC falls from 0.67 to 0.10.
  Reporting accuracy or ROC-AUC alone hides how hard "will this go viral?" is.
- Relative to chance the model is better at spotting the top 5% (2.43× lift) than the top 50% (1.55×).
- **Dedicated classifier vs one log-shares regressor** (HistGB, ROC-AUC difference, paired 95% CI):
  0.006 [0.001, 0.011] at the median, 0.008 [0.001, 0.016] at the 75th, 0.019 [0.006, 0.033] at the
  90th and 0.026 [0.005, 0.047] at the 95th percentile. The classifier's advantage grows towards the
  tail and is detectable, though the 95th-percentile interval is wide (316 positives). For PR-AUC
  the differences are not detectable except at the median (0.021 [0.010, 0.032]). Without the
  share-derived features only the 90th-percentile gap is detectable (0.015 [0.002, 0.030]).
- The F1-optimal cutoff picked on validation transfers poorly to the tail: F1 0.17 at the 95th percentile.
- The test set has only 43.5% positives at the training median — the drift from E1 again.
- Table: `results/tables/e4_threshold.csv`, paired comparisons `e4_classifier_vs_regressor.csv`.

## E5 — Learning curves

Test ROC-AUC, mean of 5 random subsamples of the training block (all features):

| Training articles | LogReg | kNN | Random forest | HistGB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 158 | 0.571 | 0.599 | 0.644 | 0.599 |
| 951 | 0.677 | 0.678 | 0.704 | 0.680 |
| 3,171 | 0.705 | 0.686 | 0.718 | 0.703 |
| 9,514 | 0.714 | 0.690 | 0.729 | 0.723 |
| 31,714 | 0.717 | 0.693 | 0.735 | 0.737 |

- Random forest is best below ~10k articles; HistGB overtakes only with the full data.
- Logistic regression and kNN plateau after ~3k articles (+0.01 from 3k to 32k); the tree
  ensembles are still improving at 32k.
- HistGB fits its training data almost perfectly up to 3k articles (train AUC ≈ 1.0), a textbook
  high-variance regime; logistic regression's train and test AUC converge by ~3k.
- With full data, logistic regression's test AUC (0.717) is *higher* than its training AUC (0.703):
  newer articles are slightly easier to rank, consistent with E1's monthly curve.

## E6 — Regularisation

- With 28k training articles the penalty hardly matters: validation AUC 0.725–0.736 for any
  L2 C ≥ 1e-4 and any L1 C ≥ 0.01.
- With 1,000 training articles it does: L2 validation AUC rises from 0.679 (C = 100) to 0.709
  (C = 0.003); L1 peaks at 0.714 (C = 0.03, 12 non-zero features). Regularisation matters exactly
  where E5 shows the variance problem.
- The sparsest useful L1 model (C = 0.001, 7 features, validation AUC 0.697) keeps: four channel
  dummies (entertainment, world, tech, social media), `kw_avg_avg`, `is_weekend`, `LDA_02`.
- kNN: validation AUC rises from 0.58 (k = 1) to 0.714 (k = 150–500) and then flattens; the
  default k = 50 is slightly under-smoothed.
